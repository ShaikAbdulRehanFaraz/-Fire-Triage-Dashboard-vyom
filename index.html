🧭 Field verification guide

A practical next-step checklist

Use these checks to decide what to investigate next. A heat detection alone does not confirm a wildfire.

📍
1. Confirm the location

Copy the selected latitude and longitude. Check whether the point is on land, near infrastructure, or in an area where heat sources are expected.

📏
2. Check the fire radius

Review the 5-meter rule. If radius ≥ 5m, investigate as an active alert. If < 5m, monitor as a sub-threshold spot.

🕒
3. Check when it was observed

Review the UTC timestamp and data age. An old observation may no longer describe current conditions.

🛰️
4. Verify independently

Compare with Satellite Image Map view, recent imagery, official fire reports, and local authorities before dispatching responders.

Safety reminder: If there is an immediate, credible threat, follow local emergency procedures. Do not approach a suspected fire to verify it yourself.
Data and limitations: Area names are the nearest city from a built-in list of about 200 major cities (works offline), so they are approximate. Demo records are generated locally and are clearly marked synthetic. Uploaded records are not sent to a server. NASA FIRMS data can include false positives and missed detections; clouds, sensor coverage, product choice, and observation time matter. Follow official NASA FIRMS documentation for the product you use.
